## 1. Client Situation

The client oversees food-safety compliance across a large and diverse portfolio of food businesses.

Current reporting is fragmented across establishment lists, inspection outcomes, rating updates and local-authority summaries. Senior management wants a clearer view of where low ratings, stale inspections, recurring compliance concerns and workload pressure are concentrated.

The organisation also wants a more consistent analytical basis for prioritising regulatory attention without treating the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme as a complete measure of current risk.

## 2. Business Problem

The organisation does not currently have one governed analytical view connecting:

- establishment characteristics;
- business type;
- food hygiene rating;
- rating date;
- component scores where available;
- local authority;
- geography;
- new-rating-pending status;
- inspection recency.

The project team has been commissioned to develop a reproducible regulatory-intelligence solution that improves management visibility and supports prioritisation.

## 3. Project Objectives

### A. Establish a trusted analytical foundation
Profile and validate the FSA/FHRS source data and document important limitations.

### B. Build a reusable regulatory analytics layer
Create reproducible structures covering establishment, rating, local-authority and geographic analysis.

### C. Define compliance and workload KPIs
Develop clear metrics around rating distribution, low-rating establishments, inspection recency, pending ratings and local-authority caseload.

### D. Identify concentrations of concern
Investigate where lower ratings, older inspections and recurring management-confidence issues are concentrated.

### E. Support inspection-prioritisation analysis
Develop transparent, evidence-based indicators that may support further review without replacing statutory inspection judgement.

### F. Produce decision-support outputs
Provide senior stakeholders with a management dashboard, concise briefing and technical handover.

## 4. Key Business Questions

1. How are food hygiene ratings distributed across business types and local authorities?
2. Where are low-rated establishments concentrated geographically?
3. Which establishments have the oldest rating dates?
4. Which business types show the highest proportion of low ratings?
5. How do hygiene, structural and confidence-in-management scores differ where component scores are available?
6. Which local authorities have the largest apparent regulatory workload?
7. Where are new ratings pending?
8. Which establishments may warrant further review because of a combination of low rating and inspection age?
9. Which KPIs should management monitor routinely?
10. What important limitations prevent the data from being used as a standalone risk score?

## 5. Expected Analytical Work

Teams are expected to:

- profile the source files/API outputs;
- validate establishment identifiers;
- standardise geography and rating fields;
- create reproducible ingestion and transformation logic;
- assess missing values and scheme differences;
- create inspection-recency measures;
- analyse rating distribution;
- compare business types and local authorities;
- perform geospatial analysis where practical;
- develop transparent prioritisation indicators;
- clearly separate evidence from enforcement judgement;
- build a stakeholder-facing dashboard/product;
- provide technical handover documentation.

## 6. Constraints

- FHRS ratings are not equivalent to a complete current food-safety risk assessment;
- rating dates may be old for legitimate operational reasons;
- schemes differ between England/Wales/Northern Ireland and Scotland;
- component scores may not be available for every record;
- raw source data must remain unchanged;
- geographic analysis must avoid misleading conclusions from establishment density alone;
- prioritisation indicators must remain interpretable;
- teams must not present analytical flags as formal enforcement decisions.

## 7. Success Criteria

The engagement will be successful where the team can demonstrate:

- reliable source ingestion;
- correct handling of ratings and schemes;
- reproducible transformations;
- defensible KPI definitions;
- meaningful inspection-recency analysis;
- responsible geographic comparison;
- transparent prioritisation logic;
- useful stakeholder reporting;
- sufficient technical documentation;
- visible individual contribution evidence.

## 8. Final Review

The team will present to a Mettelo review panel acting as the client's stakeholder group.

The panel may challenge:

- rating interpretation;
- handling of rating dates;
- local-authority comparisons;
- geographic denominators;
- prioritisation logic;
- missing data;
- scheme differences;
- recommendations;
- limitations.
